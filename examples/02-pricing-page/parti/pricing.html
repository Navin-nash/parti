Deployment questions

The six we are asked before every contract

Can Meridian run our Part 135 operation alongside the 121 certificate?

Yes, on Network. The two certificates sit on one board with a wall between them: a dispatcher assigned to the 121 desk cannot release a 135 leg, and the legality engine applies the rule set that belongs to each certificate rather than the stricter of the two. Bays and colours are shared so a supervisor watching both does not have to re-learn the board.

What happens when a feed drops in the middle of a shift?

The ribbon at the top of the board changes state and every strip whose data is older than 90 seconds is marked stale with the age printed in the field, rather than continuing to show a number that is quietly wrong. Manual entry opens on the affected fields so the desk keeps working. Nothing is silently interpolated — a dispatcher acting on a stale ETD is a worse outcome than a dispatcher who can see the feed is down.

Does Meridian write back to crew scheduling, or is it read-only?

Read-only by default on every tier. Write-back to AIMS, Sabre CrewTrac and Merlot is available on Board and Network, scoped to the specific swap you confirm, and always paired with a notification to the crew scheduler rather than a silent update. Several carriers deliberately keep it read-only: the handoff to a human scheduler is where their process wants a second pair of eyes.

How long before a dispatcher is faster on Meridian than on what they use now?

Two shifts for the rack, roughly two weeks for the recovery workflow. The field order on a strip deliberately matches a printed progress strip, so most of the muscle memory transfers on day one. The part that takes longer is trusting the legality filter enough to stop double-checking it by hand — that is a habit, not an interface problem, and we do not pretend otherwise.

Our ops specs put changes under configuration control. How do you ship?

Network deployments are pinned to a release you accept, not to our latest build. You get release notes 30 days ahead, a staging board loaded with your own schedule, and a written statement of any change that touches release generation, legality calculation or record retention. Security fixes ship on a separate track with 72 hours notice.

We fly a seasonal peak. Do we pay for desks we staff four months a year?

No. Desk tier bills the peak count of named desks in each month, so a summer ramp costs summer money. Board tier includes 12 desks flat and most seasonal carriers sit inside that without changing anything. What we do not do is hourly or per-leg pricing — it makes the bill spike on exactly the days your operation is already having its worst week.
