The first four minutes of an AOG

Not a feature list. This is the sequence Meridian runs on the mechanical above, timed from the ACARS message hitting the desk.

T+0:00

The board flags it before anyone calls maintenance

An ACARS maintenance message from N417CR posts HYD 2 LO QTY at the gate. Meridian marks the tail AOG and every remaining leg assigned to it changes state on the rack — tinted stock, a printed MECH token, and a signed delta. Three signals, so the change survives being read in peripheral vision.

T+0:20

Downstream is already drawn

The three legs the tail was flying for the rest of the day are grouped as one consequence chain — CR2208 to BOI, CR2245 back to PDX, CR1592 to YKM — with the booked load and the misconnect exposure at each stop. You are solving one problem, not discovering it three times.

T+1:10

Legal crew, not merely available crew

Every candidate is run through FAR 117 flight-duty and rest limits before it is offered. A tail whose crew would time out mid-sequence is shown struck through with the timeout hour, not hidden — you can see that Meridian considered it, which is the difference between a filter and a black box.

T+3:40

Release, notify, and leave a reason for the next shift

Assigning N731CG rewrites the flight release, pushes the change to the station and to crew scheduling, and stamps the leg with who decided what, when, and why. At handover the incoming dispatcher reads the rationale rather than reconstructing it.
