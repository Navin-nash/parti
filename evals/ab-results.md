# Blind A/B — results

**Run 2026-09-09 · seed 11 · 3 briefs · 2 arms each**

> **Headline preference number: not yet collected. 0 raters.**
> The protocol in [`README.md`](README.md) requires 5 or more human raters who did not
> build the skill. That number cannot be produced by the model that generated both arms
> and knows which is which, and publishing a self-judged figure in its place would be
> `NG-UNCHECKED-CLAIM` — the failure the gate exists to prevent. The harness is built and

What *was* run is the measured half. Those numbers are real and are below.

---

## Method

Three briefs, chosen to span archetypes and written before any output existed:

| Brief | Register | Archetype |
|---|---|---|
| `b1-reconcile` | product | data tool |
| `b2-clinic` | product | regulated |
| `b3-kiln` | brand | marketing |

Two arms per brief. **Baseline** is the same model answering the same prompt with the
skill disabled, written in good faith — a plausible, tidy answer of the kind a capable
model gives unprompted. **parti** is the same brief with the skill applied.

Reproduce with:

```bash
python evals/ab/run_ab.py --seed 11
```

## The measured half

`lint.py` and `motion.py` over each arm in isolation. Findings, not scores.

| Brief | Arm | Findings | P0 | P1 | P2 |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| b1-reconcile | baseline | 10 | 4 | 6 | 0 |
| b1-reconcile | **parti** | **1** | 1 | 0 | 0 |
| b2-clinic | baseline | 9 | 3 | 6 | 0 |
| b2-clinic | **parti** | **1** | 1 | 0 | 0 |
| b3-kiln | baseline | 11 | 3 | 7 | 1 |
| b3-kiln | **parti** | **1** | 1 | 0 | 0 |

**Totals: baseline 30 findings, parti 3.** Every arm carries exactly one, the same one,
discussed below.

Present in all three baseline arms and none of the parti arms:

- `default_violet` — the indigo accent, in all three, unprompted. This is the clearest
  single result in the run: given no direction, the model reached for the same colour
  three times out of three, across a bank tool, a health clinic, and a kiln manufacturer.
- `easing-transition-all` (P0) — `transition: all`.
- `a11y-ungated-hover` — hover styling with no pointer gate.
- `ghost_card_stack` — hairline border plus wide diffuse shadow on the same element.
- `inter_geist_only` (b1, b2) — Inter as the only named family.
- `generic_cta_pair` (b3) — "Get Started" next to "Learn More".

### What this does and does not show

It shows the skill removes the mechanically detectable defects. It does **not** show the
designs are better, and the doctrine in `README.md` applies at full strength: these
detectors were written by the same project, so a clean run means nothing on the known
list is wrong. That is a floor, not a verdict. **Do not cite 30-vs-3 as a quality
result.** It is a compliance result.

### The finding in every arm

`a11y-no-reduced-motion` (P0) fires on all six files. Both arms animate only colour, and
the honest reduced-motion form of a colour crossfade is the same crossfade — there is
nothing spatial to reduce, as argued in `foundations.md`. Adding a media query that
switches a 120ms colour change to 0ms would clear the finding and change nothing for a
user, which is precisely the Goodhart move this repo warns about. It is left firing, in
both arms, so it cancels out of the comparison.

## Defects the harness caught in the skill's own arm

Recorded because a report that only lists the other side's problems is not a test.

1. **`height: 100vh`** in `b1-reconcile/arm-parti` — a direct violation of the skill's own
   ship-floor rule, caught by `lint.py` as `h_screen`.
2. **Em dashes** in the `<title>` of all three parti arms, caught as `emdash`.
3. **A copy-versus-data contradiction** in `b3-kiln/arm-parti`: the headline read "Nine
   degrees" while the readings beside it showed seven. No script catches this. It was
   found by opening the page and looking at it, and on a page whose entire argument is
   "trust our measurements", it was the worst defect in the run.

Finding 3 is the useful one. The scripts caught two mechanical slips; the thing that
would actually have lost the sale was invisible to them and needed a human-style read.
That is the whole argument for why the preference panel is not optional.

## Rating page

`evals/ab/rate.html` is generated by the run. Sides are counterbalanced so each arm takes
the left slot an equal number of times, arms are copied to neutral `opt-1` / `opt-2`
filenames so nothing in the DOM says which is which, and the page never loads the key.
The mapping lives in `evals/ab/key.json`, which the page does not read.

The single question is "which of these would you rather ship", not "which is better
designed" — the second invites theorising, and shipping is a decision with consequences
that people answer more honestly.

**To finish this test:** serve `evals/ab/`, send `rate.html` to five or more people who
did not build the skill, collect the JSON block each one produces, and record the tally
here against the thresholds in `README.md` — above 65% is earning its context, below 60%
is not, and above 80% means checking the baseline is not a straw man.

## Two flaws found in the harness itself

Both would have quietly invalidated the result, and both were found by running the thing
rather than by reading it.

1. **The rating page leaked the answer.** The iframe `src` read
   `pairs/b1-reconcile/arm-parti.html`, visible in the DOM to any rater who looked. The
   people most likely to inspect are the ones whose judgement you most want. Arms are now
   copied to neutral `opt-1` / `opt-2` filenames at assembly time.
2. **A free shuffle is not counterbalancing.** With three pairs, seed 11 put the baseline
   on the left all three times, so any side preference a rater has would have ridden
   straight into the result. Sides are now counterbalanced, with the coin only deciding
   which arm starts.

## Threats to validity, stated up front

- **n = 3 briefs.** The protocol asks for 6 to 8. Three is enough to demonstrate the
  harness and not enough to conclude anything.
- **Same author, both arms.** I wrote the baseline knowing what the skill would do.
  I aimed for a good-faith baseline and the reader should assume some unconscious bias
  toward the arm I was arguing for. An independent baseline generator would be stronger.
- **Static pages.** No interaction, no data at real volume, no responsive check. The
  reconciliation screen is judged at four visible rows when the brief says four thousand.
- **The detectors are ours.** The 30-vs-3 gap is partly a measure of how well the skill
  complies with rules the same repo wrote.
